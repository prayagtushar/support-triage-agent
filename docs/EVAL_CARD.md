# Eval card — support triage routing

Following the shape of [Model Cards for Model Reporting](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993).
The numbers live in the [README](../README.md#evaluation), regenerated by
`make readme-metrics`; this card says what they mean and where they stop applying.

## The domain it assumes

**A consumer e-commerce mobile app, operating in India, serving English and Hinglish.**

This is stated because the eight intents only make sense together under it: a pure retailer
never receives a feature request, and a pure SaaS product never ships a package. The
implied business does both, which is ordinary for a shopping app.

It is also stated because it was not chosen up front, and that shows in the corpus. The
retrieval corpus is Bitext's, whose 27 intents are all a storefront's — `place_order`,
`track_order`, `delivery_period`, `change_shipping_address`, `check_invoice`, `get_refund`.
Six of the eight intents fall straight out of it. The remaining two were added to the
taxonomy and then had to be generated, because nothing in Bitext answers them:

| Intent | Real cases | Synthetic |
|---|---|---|
| billing, refund, account_access, shipping, how_to, other | 500 each | 10 each |
| `bug_report` | **0** | 180 |
| `feature_request` | **0** | 160 |

The domain is now a setting (`DOMAIN`) rather than a phrase inside one prompt. Both the
classifier and the drafter are told what business they are working for, because "my order has
not arrived" means different things to a retailer and a bank, and the drafter was never told
at all. `GET /policy` reports it, so the assumption the numbers were measured under is
readable rather than inferred.

Retrieval also reports the provenance of its own evidence. A result whose every case was
generated sets `synthetic_only`, which reaches the reviewer as a warning on the ticket and
marks each generated case in the evidence list. It deliberately does **not** change the route:
doing that would alter the policy the numbers on this card were measured under, and it should
be a hard rule only after a run that measures it.

Anyone adopting this should still pick the domain first and let the taxonomy and the corpus
follow from it, rather than inheriting a taxonomy from whichever dataset was convenient.

## What the system decides

Given one inbound support ticket, it produces an intent, an urgency, a language, a drafted
reply grounded in retrieved resolved cases, a judge score for that draft, and a route: answer
without a human, send to a human review queue, or escalate.

The route is the output that matters. Everything upstream exists to inform it.

## Intended use

- Triaging inbound support tickets for a team that already has a human review queue.
- Deciding **which** tickets a human sees, and in what order — not replacing that human.
- Running behind an existing helpdesk, with delivery, identity and ticket state owned elsewhere.

## Out-of-scope use

- **Auto-replying at the precision this corpus supports.** Measured precision is 0.500
  (95% CI 0.24–0.76) against a 0.95 design target. Auto-reply should stay off until refitted
  on your own data.
- **Anything with account access.** The drafter has no tools and cannot look up an order, a
  refund status or a transaction. Tickets needing that must escalate, and the `not_answerable`
  reject reason exists to catch the ones that do not.
- **Regulated advice** — medical, legal, financial.
- **Real tickets without redaction.** Every body is sent to three third-party providers.
- **Languages beyond English and Hinglish.** Devanagari rests on a single ticket.

## Evaluation data

| | |
|---|---|
| Golden set | `v0`, 60 hand-written tickets |
| Composition | 44 English, 15 Hinglish, 1 Devanagari; 5 adversarial |
| Retrieval corpus | ~3,400 resolved cases: 3,000 [Bitext](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bitext/Bitext-customer-support-llm-chatbot-training-dataset) mapped from 27 intents onto 8, ~400 generated |
| Labels | Intent, urgency, language and correct route, hand-assigned |
| Not verified | The Hinglish set has not been reviewed by a native speaker |
| Synthetic | `bug_report` and `feature_request` have no Bitext cases at all; both intents, and 80 Hinglish cases, are generated and marked `source='synthetic'` |

## Metrics, and why these ones

- **Auto-reply precision** — of the replies sent without a human, how many were right. The
  only metric where being wrong reaches a customer, so it leads.
- **Review recall** — of the tickets that needed a human, how many got one. The silent
  failure mode.
- **Routing accuracy** — reported, but it averages the two above and hides both.
- **Calibration** — whether a stated confidence of 0.86 means anything, given the router
  consumes that number as truth.
- **Cost and p95 latency** — the operating constraints.

Not reported: BLEU, ROUGE or any n-gram overlap against a reference reply. There is no single
correct reply to a support ticket, and a metric that rewards resembling one would be measuring
the wrong thing.

## Caveats

1. **Two intents have no real cases behind them.** `bug_report` and `feature_request` are
   grounded entirely in generated text. Drafts for those intents retrieve LLM-written cases
   to answer LLM-written tickets, scored by an LLM judge. `feature_request` reports precision
   1.000, recall 1.000 and F1 1.000 on a support of 6 — that figure measures how alike two
   generation processes are, not how well the system handles a feature request. Read both
   intents as untested rather than perfect.
2. **n is the binding constraint.** Auto-reply precision is measured on about ten tickets at
   the shipped threshold. Two identical runs returned 0.778 and 0.500. `make coverage` puts
   the set size needed to resolve it to ±0.05 at ~120 tickets.
3. **Confidence is overconfident in every bucket.** The composite weights (0.5 judge, 0.3
   classifier, 0.2 retrieval) were a guess, and two of the three inputs are optimistic by
   construction.
4. **The judge-only arm beats the shipped composite** in both runs. It has not been adopted
   because whether the judge is strictly necessary flips sign between runs at n=60, and
   retuning on noise makes a policy worse.
5. **The corpus limits the ceiling.** Bitext's resolutions are largely templates, so retrieval
   returns topically similar cases containing no answer to ground on. 25 of 60 drafts declared
   themselves unable to answer while only 5 had weak retrieval — and were not wrong to.
6. **The voice numbers are a floor, and none of the metrics above were measured on them.**
   Every latency in [`VOICE.md`](VOICE.md) was produced by synthesising the golden set with
   text-to-speech and feeding it back in. That audio is cleaner than any caller on a real
   line: no crosstalk, no clipping, no traffic, no accent the recogniser has not heard. It
   is also the same clip for every arm, which is what makes the arms comparable, so the
   trade is deliberate. Read those latencies as the best case and speech recognition
   accuracy as untested.

   Two of the metrics on this page stop applying to the streamed arms outright. Those arms
   give up the drafter's JSON schema, so `citations` and `is_safe_fallback` are gone and the
   router decides on one signal fewer than the composite was measured under. The routing
   numbers here were measured on the text path and have not been re-established for voice.

7. **The models are hosted and they drift, so every number here has a date on it.**
   On 2026-08-01 the drafter produced 60 drafts from 60 golden tickets inside a 4096 token
   budget. On 2026-09-03, same model name, same budget, same golden set, it produced 37 and
   returned nothing on the other 23: its reasoning had grown past the ceiling, and a
   truncated reply is empty rather than short. Nothing in this repository changed in
   between. The budget is now 16384 and the numbers on this page were re-measured under it.

   Read that as a property of the system rather than an incident. Any metric here is a
   measurement of a specific model on a specific day, and a hosted model is free to change
   under a stable name. `make gate` compares each run against the stored baseline for
   exactly this reason, and it is the check most worth running on a schedule rather than
   before a change.

8. **Every number on this page is the e-commerce desk's.** A second desk exists and is
   labelled `provenance='synthetic'`: all 436 of its cases were generated by a model, so a
   draft there is machine text grounded in machine text and graded by a third machine. It
   is in the product to show that the taxonomy, the corpus and the retrieval scope are
   genuinely per desk, and retrieval is enforced not to cross between them.

   It is not evaluated. There is no golden set for it, none of the metrics above were
   measured on it, and caveat 1's warning about the two synthetic e-commerce intents
   applies to the whole desk rather than to two rows of a table. Read anything it produces
   as a demonstration of routing, never as a claim about tech support. The switcher, the
   desk card and a banner above its queue all say so, because a full queue on a generated
   corpus is indistinguishable from a full queue on a real one.

9. **A quiet failure exists and is monitored.** When the embedding key ran out of credit,
   every retrieval returned nothing, every ticket was correctly routed to a human by hard rule,
   and no conventional signal moved. `GET /status` reports the empty-retrieval rate for this
   reason.

## Ethical and operational considerations

- **Data residency.** Sarvam is India-based; the other two providers are not.
- **Human override is the design, not a fallback.** Every non-auto-reply route lands in a
  queue where a person approves, edits or rejects, and every decision is appended to an audit
  trail with the confidence the agent reported at that moment.
- **Rejects are labelled, not free text.** A reviewer picks from a fixed taxonomy, so the
  disagreements accumulate as evaluation data instead of as comments.

## Recommendation

Run it for triage and ordering. Leave auto-reply off until it has been refitted on your own
cases and the golden set is large enough for the precision figure to mean something.
